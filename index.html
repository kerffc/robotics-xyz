Learn: robot hardware in three parts

A quick primer if you're new to the space — every robot's hardware boils down to three cooperating systems.

01

Movement

Motors alone just spin. A servo pairs a motor with a position sensor and a small controller so it can hold a target angle, constantly comparing "where am I" against "where should I be" and correcting the gap — the same loop (often a PID controller) shows up anywhere a robot has to hit and hold a position. Between the motor and the joint sits a gearbox: it trades speed for torque, so a fast, weak motor becomes a slow, strong joint.

02

Body

The frame and joints set the robot's physical limits — reach, stability, load capacity — and how many independent ways it can move (its degrees of freedom). The body isn't passive: if it flexes or vibrates, the robot's internal sense of where its own parts are stops matching reality. Material choice (plastic for fast prototyping, aluminum or carbon fiber for strength and stiffness) is a real engineering tradeoff, not just cosmetics.

03

Sensors

Sensors split into two jobs: proprioceptive ones look inward (encoders for joint angle, an IMU for orientation), and exteroceptive ones look outward (cameras, depth/LiDAR, touch). A robot needs both — seeing an object is only useful once the robot also knows where its own arm is relative to it. None of this works without calibration, which maps raw sensor numbers into one shared coordinate system the robot can trust.

Simplified summary — for the full technical deep-dive (PID tuning, transmission types, sensor fusion, a real hardware teardown), read the original at Interlatent.
